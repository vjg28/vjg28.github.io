Astrophotography

During COVID I spent clear nights on the roof at home in Gujarat with a Celestron AstroMaster 130EQ, a 130 mm reflector telescope. I didn't have a proper camera for it, so I held my phone up to the eyepiece and hoped the stars would line up. These are the ones that came out.

Where they are

Everything I photographed, drawn where it really sits in the winter sky from home. Tap or hover over one to jump to its photos.

Stars, star clusters and a galaxy

Closer to home (these move around, so they're not on the chart)

Brighter stars are drawn bigger, and the lines join them into constellations like Orion, Taurus and Gemini.

The photos

  1. 23 Dec 2020

    Jupiter and Saturn, together

    Two days after the great conjunction, the two planets were still close enough to fit in one frame. Saturn's rings are visible, and so are Jupiter's moons, strung out in a line.

    What
    The two biggest planets, side by side in the sky
    Last this close
    1623
    Taken with
    Phone held to the telescope
  2. 29 Dec 2020

    First frames

    Before aiming at anything, I just wanted to see if my phone could pick up stars at all. Countably infinite, it turns out.

    What
    A patch of stars
    Taken with
    My phone
  3. 31 Dec 2020

    Andromeda Galaxy

    I posted this one as a puzzle first: can you find the galaxy? It's the faint oval smudge, and Mars happened to be in the shot too. The light in that smudge left Andromeda about 2.5 million years ago.

    What
    Our nearest big galaxy
    How far
    About 2.5 million light-years
    Also in the shot
    Mars
  4. 31 Dec 2020 · again 4 Mar 2021

    Pleiades

    The Seven Sisters are easy to see with your own eyes and surprisingly hard to catch on a phone. I tried twice. The second try came out so faint that I turned it into a meme.

    What
    A cluster of young stars
    How far
    About 444 light-years
  5. 31 Dec 2020

    Moon halo

    The first time I caught one. The ring isn't out in space at all. It's moonlight bent by tiny ice crystals in thin, high cloud.

    What
    Ice crystals in the clouds
  6. 13 Jan 2021 · again 4 Mar 2021

    Orion Nebula

    My favourite. In Hubble's photos it's a huge swirl of red and purple gas. Through my telescope it's a small grey-green glow around a few bright stars. Both are real. Mine is a single photo, straight off the phone with no editing.

    I captioned one of these "Observing the past", and I meant it: the light I caught left the nebula about 1,344 years ago.

    What
    A cloud of glowing gas where stars are born
    How far
    About 1,344 light-years
    Taken with
    Phone held to the telescope
  7. 4 Mar 2021

    Sirius

    The brightest star in the night sky, and one of the closest. In the photo it's a hard white point that wouldn't sit still in the eyepiece.

    What
    A bright white star
    How far
    8.6 light-years
  8. 4 Mar 2021

    Betelgeuse

    Orion's red shoulder. Even on a phone it comes out orange. A year earlier it had dimmed so much that people wondered if it was about to explode. By this night it was back to normal.

    What
    A huge, cool red star
    How far
    Roughly 550 light-years
  9. 4 Mar 2021

    Beehive Cluster

    It sits in Cancer, a faint constellation that's hard to spot from the city. Through the telescope it breaks into a loose swarm of stars, which is how it got its name.

    What
    A loose cluster of stars
    How far
    About 577 light-years
  10. 4 Mar 2021

    The Moon

    The easy one that never gets old. The craters look sharpest along the edge between light and shadow, where the sunlight comes in low.

    What
    Ours
    How far
    About 384,400 km
  11. 17 Dec 2021

    Venus

    I went out to find a comet and didn't. Venus was low in the evening sky that week, so I got it instead. Like the Moon, it goes through phases, and here it's a clear crescent.

    What
    Planet
    Shape that night
    Crescent
